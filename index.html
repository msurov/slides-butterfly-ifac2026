The robot dynamics are described by the Euler–Lagrange equations $$ M\!\left(q\right)\ddot{q}+C\left(q,\dot{q}\right)\dot{q}+G\left(q\right) = F(q) u, \quad q \in \mathbb{R}^2, \quad u \in \mathbb{R} $$ where
$q$ generalized coordinates
$\dot q \equiv \frac{dq}{dt}$, $\ddot q \equiv \frac{d^2q}{dt^2}$ generalized velocities and acceletations
$u$ control input
$M(q) \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}$ positive definite symmetric matrix
$C(q, \dot q) \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}$ a matrix function which is linear in $\dot q$
$G(q) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ contains potential forces
$F(q) \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$ is a nonzero vector field
The system admits a periodic solution $$ q_\star(t + T) = q_\star(t), \quad u_\star(t + T) = u_\star(t), \quad T > 0. $$
Problem: Construct a state-feedback controller $$u = u(q, \dot q),$$ such that the closed-loop system $$ M\left(q\right)\ddot{q}+C\left(q,\dot{q}\right)\dot{q}+G\left(q\right) = F(q) \, u(q, \dot q) $$ renders $$ x_\star(t) = \bigl(q_\star(t),\dot q_\star(t)\bigr) $$ a solution that is asymptotically orbitally stable.

A common approach to stabilizing $\mathrm{orb}\,x_\star$ is based on the transverse linearization.

We define new coordinates in a tubular vicinity of $\mathrm{orb} x_\star$:
  1. Introduce transverse coordinates $$ \xi = \beta \left(q,\dot{q}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3} $$ and a projection variable identifies the closest point on the orbit $$ \theta = \pi \left(q,\dot{q}\right)\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} $$ Along the desired trajectory, $$\begin{aligned} \beta\left(q_{\star}\left(t\right),\dot{q}_{\star}\left(t\right)\right)&=0, \\ \frac{d}{dt}\pi\left(q_{\star}\left(t\right),\dot{q}_{\star}\left(t\right)\right)&>0\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R} \end{aligned}$$ Locally, the transformation $$ (q,\dot q)\mapsto(\theta,\xi) $$ is a diffeomorphism.
  1. Introduce a control transformation $$ u=u(q,\dot q,w), $$ such that the system takes the form $$ \ddot q=f(q,\dot q)+g(q)w, $$ with the desired trajectory corresponding to $$ w = 0, \qquad \ddot q_\star(t) = f(q_\star(t), \dot q_\star(t)). $$
  2. Applying the coordinate and control transformations, and using $\theta$ as the independent variable, $$ \frac{d}{dt}=\left(\frac{\partial\pi}{\partial q}\dot{q}+\frac{\partial\pi}{\partial\dot{q}}f\left(q,\dot{q}\right)+\frac{\partial\pi}{\partial\dot{q}}g\left(q\right)w\right)\frac{d}{d\theta}, $$ the dynamics take the form $$ \frac{d\xi}{d\theta} = A(\theta)\xi+B(\theta)w+o(\xi,w), $$ where $A(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ and $B(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ are periodic.

    This is the transverse linearization.

  1. Choose a feedback $$ w=K(\theta)\xi, \qquad K(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times(n-1)}, $$ such that the LTV system $$ \frac{d\bar\xi}{d\theta} = \bigl(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)\bigr)\bar\xi $$ is exponentially stable. This yields the state feedback
    $$ u=u\bigl(q,\dot q,K(\theta)\xi\bigr), \qquad \theta=\pi(q,\dot q),\quad \xi=\beta(q,\dot q), $$
    which orbitally stabilizes $\mathrm{orb}\,x_\star$.
  2. A standard approach for computing $K(\theta)$ is linear-quadratic regulation (LQR), which requires solving a periodic matrix Riccati differential equation.
Is there an alternative to LQR?
For any stabilizing feedback $w = K(\theta)\xi$, there exist weighting matrices making it an LQR solution.

Can we construct a different class of stabilizing controllers?

The LTV system possesses special structure

Let $h:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfy $$ h(q)=0,\qquad dh(q)M^{-1}(q)F(q)\neq0 \quad \forall q\in\mathrm{orb}\,x_\star. $$ Define the transverse coordinates $\xi=\beta(x)$ by $$ \xi_1=h(q),\qquad \xi_2=L_{\dot q}h(q),\qquad \xi_3=\beta_3(q,\dot q), $$ where $d\beta_3$ is linearly independent of $dh$ and $dL_{\dot q}h$. The control transformation $u\mapsto w$ is defined as $$ \begin{aligned} w={}&dh(q)M^{-1}(q) \bigl(-C(q,\dot q)\dot q-G(q)+F(q)u\bigr)\\ &+L_{\dot q}^2h(q)+k_1h(q)+k_2L_{\dot q}h(q), \end{aligned} $$ where $k_1,k_2>0$.
The transverse linearization has the form $$ \frac{d\bar{\xi}}{d\theta}=\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}{ccc} 0 & 1 & 0\\ -k_{1} & -k_{2} & 0\\ a_{1}\left(\theta\right) & a_{2}\left(\theta\right) & a_{3}\left(\theta\right) \end{array}\right)}_{=:A\left(\theta\right)}\bar{\xi}+\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}{c} 0\\ 1\\ b\left(\theta\right) \end{array}\right)}_{=:B\left(\theta\right)}w. $$ The functions $a_i(\theta)$ and $b(\theta)$ are periodic with the same period.
This special structure implies important properties of the LTV system.
Proposition:
For any $k_{1},k_{2}>0$, the unforced $T$-periodic LTV system \[ \small \frac{d\bar{\xi}}{d\theta}=\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}{ccc} 0 & 1 & 0\\ -k_{1} & -k_{2} & 0\\ a_{1}\left(\theta\right) & a_{2}\left(\theta\right) & a_{3}\left(\theta\right) \end{array}\right)}_{=:A\left(\theta\right)}\bar{\xi} \] possesses at least two stable Floquet multipliers $\left|\mu_{1,2}\right|<1$.
Equivalently, let $X(\theta)$ be the solution of the matrix initial-value problem \[ \small \frac{dX}{d\theta} = A(\theta) X, \quad X(0) = I_{3\times 3}, \quad \theta \in [0, T]. \] The monodromy matrix $\Psi \equiv X(T)$ has (at least) two eigenvalues satisfying $|\mu_{1,2}| < 1$.
Corollary:
For any $k_{1},k_{2}>0$, the unforced system possesses a two-dimensional invariant subspace $$ \mathcal{S}_{\theta} \equiv \mathrm{span}\{ e_{1}(\theta), e_{2}(\theta) \}. $$ spanned solutions $\bar{\xi}=e_{1}\left(\theta\right)$ and $\bar{\xi}=e_{2}\left(\theta\right)$, associated with the stable Floquet multipliers $\mu_1, \mu_2$, provided $\mu_1 \ne \mu_2$. Consequently, any trajectory initialized in $\mathcal{S}_{\theta_{0}}$ remains in $S_{\theta}$ for all $\theta\geq\theta_{0}$ and converges exponentially to the origin as $\theta\to\infty.$
Proof:
The monodromy matrix has the lower block-triangular form \[ \Psi=\left(\begin{array}{cc} \exp\left(\begin{array}{cc} 0 & T\\ -k_{1}T & -k_{2}T \end{array}\right) & \begin{array}{c} 0\\ 0 \end{array}\\ \Psi_{31}\quad\quad\quad\Psi_{32} & \Psi_{33} \end{array}\right). \] The spectrum of a block-triangular matrix is the union of the spectra of the diagonal blocks. Hence, $$ \mu_{1,2}=\exp\left(\frac{-k_{2}\pm\sqrt{k_{2}^{2}-4k_{1}}}{2}T\right),\quad\mu_{3}=\Psi_{33}. $$ Since $k_{1},k_{2}>0$ and $T>0$, it follows that $\left|\mu_{1,2}\right|<1$.
The system behavior inside $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ is stable. We must design a feedback law $w(\theta,\bar\xi)$ that drives every trajectory originating outside $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ into it in finite time.

Consider the LTV system \[ \frac{d\bar\xi}{d\theta} = A\left(\theta\right) \bar\xi + B\left(\theta\right) w \] where $\bar\xi \in \mathbb{R}^3$, $w \in \mathbb{R}$, and the continuous matrix functions $A(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ and $B(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times1}$ are $T$-periodic. Assume that the unforced system \[ \frac{d \bar\xi}{dt} = A\left(\theta\right) \bar\xi \] admits two solutions $\bar\xi = e_{1}\left(\theta\right)$, $\bar\xi = e_{2}\left(\theta\right)$, satisfying $\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e_{i}\left(\theta\right)=0$, and that for all $\theta$ the vectors $e_{1}\left(\theta\right)$, $e_{2}\left(\theta\right)$ and $B\left(\theta\right)$ are linearly independent. Define \[ z\equiv n^{\top}(\theta) \bar\xi \in\mathbb{R}, \quad \text{where} \quad n(\theta)\equiv\frac{e_{1}\left(\theta\right)\times e_{2}\left(\theta\right)}{\left\Vert e_{1}\left(\theta\right)\times e_{2}\left(\theta\right)\right\Vert }\in\mathbb{R}^{3}, \] where "$\times$" denotes the standard cross product in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Then, for any $k_3>0$, the feedback control law \[ w\left(\theta, \bar\xi\right) = -\frac{n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)A\left(\theta\right)n\left(\theta\right)z+k_3\mathrm{sign}\left(z\right)}{n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)B\left(\theta\right)}, \] renders the origin of the closed-loop system globally asymptotically stable.

Comment:
The feedback $w(\theta,\bar\xi)$ imposes the following dynamics for the sliding variable $z$: $$ \frac{dz}{d\theta} = -k_3\,\mathrm{sign}(z). $$
* The full proof in M. Surov, "Sliding-Mode-Based Control Design for the Butterfly Robot," IEEE TCST, DOI: 10.1109/TCST.2026.3654633.

Remark:
For the LTV system \[ \frac{d\bar\xi}{d\theta} = A\left(\theta\right) \bar\xi + B\left(\theta\right) w \] we have made the assumption: \[ n^\top(\theta) B(\theta) \ne 0 \] (or, equivalently, on linear independence of the vectors $e_1(\theta),$ $e_2(\theta)$ and $B(\theta)$ for all $\theta$).
If the requirement fails, then the desired dynamics $$ \frac{dz}{d\theta} = -k_3 \,\mathrm{sign}(z) $$ cannot be achieved. This issue was considered in [1].
[1] L. Ovalle, L. Fridman, "Sliding mode control for systems with zero-crossing control gain under matched and mismatched disturbances," Automatica, vol. 159, 2024.
Feedback modification:
If $n^{\top}\!\left(\theta_z\right)B\left(\theta_z\right) = 0$ at finitely many isolated points $\theta_z \in [0, T]$, we can change the feedback $$ w\left(\theta,\xi\right)=-\boxed{\frac{n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)A\left(\theta\right)n\left(\theta\right)}{n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)B\left(\theta\right)}} \, z- \boxed{\frac{k_{3}}{n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)B\left(\theta\right)}}\,\mathrm{sign}\left(z\right) $$ with $$ w\left(\theta,\xi\right)=-\boxed{k_{4}\,\sigma\left(n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)B\left(\theta\right)\right)} \, z -\boxed{k_{3}\,\sigma\left(n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)B\left(\theta\right)\right)} \, \mathrm{sign}\left(z\right) $$ where $\sigma$ is a sigmoid-like function, e.g., $$ \sigma(x) = x / (|x| + \varepsilon) \quad \text{for} \quad x \in \mathbb{R}. $$ The proposed control induces the dynamics of the sliding variable
$$\frac{dz}{d\theta}=$$ $$ \left(n^{\top}A\,n-k_{4} \, n^{\top}B\,\sigma\left(n^{\top}B\right)\right) $$ periodic function of $\theta$ $$ z - $$ $$ \quad k_{3} \, n^{\top}B\,\sigma\left(n^{\top}B\right) \quad $$ non-negative periodic function of $\theta$ $$ \mathrm{sign}\left(z\right) $$

and convergence over a period can be achieved by selecting sufficiently large $k_3, k_4.$

  1. From $$\theta = \pi(q, \dot q) \quad \text{and} \quad \ddot q = f(q, \dot q) + g(q) w$$ we obtain \[ \frac{dt}{d\theta}=\frac{1}{\partial_{q}\pi\left(q,\dot{q}\right)\dot{q}+\partial_{\dot{q}}\pi\left(q,\dot{q}\right)f\left(q,\dot{q}\right)+\partial_{\dot{q}}\pi\left(q,\dot{q}\right)g\left(q\right)w} \]
    Physical time becomes a dynamic variable in $(\theta,\xi)$ coordinates.
    Define the lateness variable $$ \delta := t - \theta^{-1}_{\star}\left(\theta\right), \quad \theta_{\star}\left(t\right) := \pi\left(q_{\star}\left(t\right),\dot{q}_{\star}\left(t\right)\right). $$
  2. The extended transverse dynamics have the linearization $$\begin{aligned} \frac{d\xi}{d\theta} &= A(\theta)\xi + B(\theta) w \\ \frac{d\delta}{d\theta} &= A_{\delta}(\theta)\xi + B_{\delta}(\theta) w \end{aligned}$$
    The lateness dynamics do not depend on $\delta$.
  1. For the LTV system, we design the feedback
    $$ w\left(\theta,\xi,\delta\right)= $$ $$ K(\theta) \xi \quad $$ stabilization term $-$ $$ \frac{k_{s} \, \mathrm{sign}\left(n^{\top}\left(\theta\right)\xi+\delta\right) }{n^{\top}\!\left(\theta\right)B\left(\theta\right)+B_{\delta}\left(\theta\right)} $$ time synchronization term

    where $k_s > 0$ and $n(\theta)$ is the periodic solution of $$ \frac{dn}{d\theta}=-\left(A\left(\theta\right)+B\left(\theta\right)K\left(\theta\right)\right)^{\top}n -\left(A_{\delta}\left(\theta\right)+B_{\delta}\left(\theta\right)K\left(\theta\right)\right)^{\top}. $$ For $k_s=0$, the controller reduces to standard orbital stabilization. Thus, $k_s$ provides a tunable trade-off between orbit tracking and synchronization.
  2. The proposed feedback provides both asymptotic orbital stability and $$ \delta\to0. $$ Consequently, $$ x(t) - x_\star(t) \to 0, $$ i.e., the desired trajectory is asymptotically stable*.
* M. Surov et. al., Orbital Stabilization and Time Synchronization of Unstable Periodic Motions in Underactuated Robots, ICRA 2026
The synchronization algorithm was verified in experiments*. The period of the trajectory is $T=4.77$ sec. The initial desynchronization is about $15$ sec.
